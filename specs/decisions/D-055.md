# D-55: Dedicated terms tools, one approval per agreement

- **Status:** Decided (14 Oct 2026); item (5) proposed, owner confirms at merge
- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Specs:** 04, 06, 11
- **Index:** [all decisions](README.md)

## Decision

**Dedicated terms tools, one approval per agreement** (product review recommendation 4, adopted by Tali on 14 Oct 2026). (1) **Two new MCP tools** (06 §2.2a): `agentlink_propose_terms` opens or revises an offer from typed `terms` fields, and `agentlink_respond_to_terms` answers a received offer with `accept`, `reject` or `counter`. The core fills `termsId`, `version`, `parties`, `previousTermsHash` and `termsHash`. An agent never computes a hash, and an accept names the received offer by envelope ID, so the agent cannot change the terms it accepts. (2) **One path per action.** `agentlink_send` no longer sends `offer`, `accept` or `reject`. `agentlink_acknowledge` keeps `accepted` and `rejected` only for records without `terms`. The frozen surface becomes 15 tools (amends D-53). (3) **One approval per agreement per owner** (04 §3.4.2). A commitment approval binds a scope: own agent, decision, `termsHash`, counterparty, thread, the answered envelope and its hash, `notarize` and the maximum fee, and an expiry fixed when the request is created: `min(validUntil, created + 7 days)`. It authorises exactly one `accept` envelope plus at most one `ACCEPTED` receipt on the answered offer's record. The receipt is signed once that record is known, before the expiry. Each part runs at most once. A newer version, lapsed terms, a changed contact identity, a `deny` rule or any change to the scope voids the approval, and then nothing is sent or signed. Auto-accept within limits (D-31) creates the same scope as a policy grant, which is void when the policy changes. Neither shape of J8 (offer recorded, or accept recorded) asks an owner twice for one agreement. (4) **Hardening from review IR-D55-1.** Terms state who pays (`price.payer`) and who delivers each item (`deliverables[].by`). Auto-accept applies only in the direction the owner chose (`autoAcceptDirection`: buy or sell) and within its limits; sell is off by default, and turning it on shows a warning that the owner commits to deliver whatever the contact writes. A second envelope for the same version from the same sender is refused, a version is locked once accepted, and concurrent counters from both sides are a conflict, with no tie-break (IR-D55-2). Commitment approvals use the owner-signed `approval.decide` and `approval.cancel` (04 §2.5.1); this section is 04 §3.4.2. Terms must be canonical (NFC, no control, bidi or invisible characters, strict amounts, currencies and dates) and are displayed from the stored bytes. Each core computes the negotiation chain from its own store, keyed per pair of parties, with no forks. `agentlink_notarize` refuses envelopes that carry `terms`. A `note` is never part of an accept or a firm offer. At most one live scope exists per answered envelope, parts are claimed atomically, and a policy grant is re-evaluated against the verified policy before each part. The owner can cancel unused parts. A plain `reject` is not final; only a `REJECTED` receipt is. (5) **Proposed; owner confirms at merge:** a firm offer (notarized offer) is a commitment (11 §14 Q1 A); the owner's signed approval goes into the proof-of-agreement file by default, with an option to leave it out (Q2 C); the expiry cap is 7 days from request creation, bounded by `validUntil`; a `REJECTED` receipt asks the owner unless the policy is explicitly `allow`. (6) **Unchanged:** D-31's default (a binding accept needs the owner's approval; auto-accept never covers `REJECTED` receipts), owner presence, I-4 (no tool decides an approval; an `approvalId` only lets the bound scope run) and I-5 (terms appear on approvals only as the counterparty's content, never in summaries).
