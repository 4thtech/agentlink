**Breaking (unreleased envelope format): key commitment and CEK-derived wraps (G-46, D-66).** The payload AEAD no longer uses the content key (CEK) as its key. `encKey` (`agentlink/v1/payload-key`) and a 32-byte key commitment `kc` (`agentlink/v1/payload-commit`) are derived from the CEK with HKDF-SHA256, and `aead` is now exactly `{alg: "A256GCM-KC", iv, kc, ct}`. The parser and `@agentlink/types`' `aeadSchema` refuse the earlier `A256GCM` form and any `kc` that is not exactly 32 bytes. `decryptPayload` compares `kc` in constant time before AES-GCM and reports a mismatch as the same `open_failed` as a failed tag. The new `checkKeyCommitment(envelope, cek)` lets the core tell the two apart. `wrapCek` is now deterministic: each entry's ephemeral X25519 key is derived from the CEK, the envelope id and the recipient key (`agentlink/v1/cek-eph`) and passed to `@hpke/core` as an imported key pair, never through `DeriveKeyPair`. The new `checkWrap(pk, id, cek, entry)` recomputes one entry from a public key and the CEK and compares it byte for byte. Derived keys are wiped after use. New exports: `checkWrap`, `checkKeyCommitment`, `KEY_COMMITMENT_LENGTH`, `PAYLOAD_KEY_LABEL`, `PAYLOAD_COMMIT_LABEL` and `CEK_EPH_LABEL`. The worst-case envelope grows from 24 509 to 24 563 bytes, still under the 24 576-byte limit.
